The problem

The tech prices the water heater, main sewer line, sump pump, or repipe and hands the homeowner a number.

The homeowner says, "We'll talk it over and let you know." Then the phone goes quiet.

Nobody owns the callback. There's no name on the follow-up and no due date on the desk — so the estimate sits in a folder, and the job goes to the shop that checked back in.

02 / The sheet

What the map is

It is one clean page built to live right beside the phone or dispatch screen. One row per open estimate, started the moment a price goes out.

  • Which plumbing estimate is open
  • Who owns the next follow-up call
  • When that callback is due
  • What happened when your office reached out

It includes a clearly marked fictional sample row and blank rows ready for your shop. Simple enough to hand to whoever answers the phone today: no name on the row, no callback happens.

03 / Daily habit

How it works

  1. An estimate goes out — start a row before the tech rolls to the next call.
  2. Assign one owner and a due date — whoever is named on the row owns the check-in.
  3. Call on or before the due date — write one line under "Outcome" so nothing falls through the cracks.
